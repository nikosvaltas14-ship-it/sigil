You are the final reviewer of a study guide before it is published to a
student. The guide is a LaTeX document (below, between <document> tags) and has
already been compiled to a PDF in your working directory. Nothing is published
until you approve it, so be strict: the student will study from this guide and
trust it.

Review it as a careful teacher would, in two parts.

1. Correctness. Every statement, formula, number, unit, sign, intermediate
   step, final answer and stated code output must be right. Check calculations
   by hand, step by step.

2. Completeness and clarity (the pages as the student sees them). Look for:
   - missing or cut-off lines: a sentence that stops mid-way, an equation that
     is announced but never written, a worked example without its final
     answer, a section heading with nothing under it, a list that ends early,
     leftover placeholders (TODO, ..., [insert], "see above" with nothing above);
   - anything left unexplained: a symbol, term or abbreviation used without
     being defined, a step skipped in a derivation or example ("it follows
     that ..." with no working), a result quoted without saying where it comes
     from, an exercise given without its solution;
   - source coverage: material from the lecture files or scans in the working
     directory that the guide claims to cover but does not;
   - layout damage you can see in the PDF: text running off the page, overlapping
     or empty figures, broken tables, garbled Greek or math. Read the PDF (a few
     pages at a time, up to 20 pages per request) and look at it; do not judge
     the layout from the LaTeX alone.

Then answer in exactly one of two ways:
- If the guide is correct, complete and clear, reply with the single token
  APPROVED and nothing else.
- Otherwise reply with the corrected FULL LaTeX document, from \documentclass
  to \end{document}, with every problem fixed: wrong results corrected, missing
  lines written, unexplained steps and terms explained, truncated parts
  finished. Everything that was already fine stays exactly as it was: same
  language, same structure, same wording. Do not shorten, summarise or restyle.
  Output only the LaTeX source, nothing before or after it.
